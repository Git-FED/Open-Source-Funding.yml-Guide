# 🛡️ Risk & Sponsor Relations

Practical tips for handling money without getting burned. Most funding guides stop at "here's how to get paid." This one covers what happens after.

---

## 1. Always keep a buffer balance

**The problem:** Chargebacks, disputes, and refunds can hit weeks after a payment lands. If you've already withdrawn everything, you go negative—and the platform may freeze your account.

**The fix:**
- Keep **at least 10–20% of your monthly income** sitting in the platform account.
- For Ko-fi and Liberapay, which pay directly to your Stripe/PayPal, keep a buffer in **those** accounts instead.
- Treat the buffer as untouchable. It's not your money—it's your insurance.

**Why it matters:** A single $500 chargeback can wipe out a month of $50 donations. The buffer absorbs the hit without disrupting your cash flow.

---

## 2. Follow up after every order or donation

**The problem:** Silence feels impersonal. Donors who don't hear back are less likely to give again.

**The fix:**
- Send a **thank-you message within 24 hours**. Even a short one.
- For recurring sponsors, follow up **monthly** with a quick update: what you shipped, what's next.
- For one-time donors, a simple "thanks—here's what your support made possible" goes a long way.

**Platform support:**
- **Patreon** has built-in messaging and post scheduling.
- **Ko-fi** lets you send thank-you messages manually or via integrations.
- **GitHub Sponsors** doesn't have built-in messaging—use email or a pinned Discussion.

---

## 3. Ask for reviews and ratings

**The problem:** Social proof drives more support, but most maintainers never ask.

**The fix:**
- After a positive interaction, ask: "If you found this useful, a star or a mention helps others find it."
- On platforms that support reviews (Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi shop), **ask directly** after delivery.
- On GitHub, ask for a **star** or a **sponsor testimonial** you can feature in your README.

**What not to do:** Don't beg. One ask per interaction. If they don't respond, move on.

---

## 4. Offer free trials or cancellation windows

**The problem:** People hesitate to commit to recurring support because they're afraid of being charged without realizing.

**The fix:**
- If your platform supports it, offer a **7-day free trial** on membership tiers.
- If it doesn't, offer a **no-questions-asked cancellation window**: "Cancel within 14 days, full refund."
- **Announce it clearly** on your funding page. The goal is to reduce friction, not hide the charge.

**Platform support:**
- **Patreon** supports free trials on membership tiers.
- **Ko-fi** memberships can be set up with a trial via integrations, but it's not native.
- **Buy Me a Coffee** supports free trials on memberships.
- **GitHub Sponsors** does **not** support trials—it's one-time or monthly, no trial period.

---

## 5. Never share external payment links randomly

**The problem:** Posting a raw PayPal.me link or a direct bank transfer request looks like a scam. It also exposes you to fraud, chargebacks, and no recourse.

**The fix:**
- **Always use a third-party platform** (GitHub Sponsors, Ko-fi, Patreon, etc.) as your primary ask.
- **Integrated platforms are safer**, even if fees are higher. They handle:
  - Payment processing
  - Fraud detection
  - Chargeback disputes
  - Tax receipts
  - Payout compliance
- **If you must share a direct link**, only do it in a private, trusted context—never in a public README or social post.

**Trade-off:** Integrated platforms take a cut, but they absorb the risk. A raw PayPal link saves 3% but exposes you to 100% of the downside.

> **Treat every donation like a customer relationship, not a transaction.**

Follow up. Say thank you. Offer a way out. Keep a buffer. Use the platform's tools instead of raw links.

That's how you build a funding stream that lasts longer than one viral moment.
